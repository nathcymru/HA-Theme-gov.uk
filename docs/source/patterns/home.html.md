# Home and rooms

The home overview answers three questions: what needs attention, what is happening and what can I control?

## Order the overview

1. Show important household alerts with their context.
2. Show frequently used controls and meaningful summaries.
3. Group rooms or tasks with clear headings.
4. Offer history and detailed settings after immediate actions.

Do not make every card equally prominent. Avoid a dense wall of gauges with no explanation of what matters.

## Room pages

Use the room name as the heading. Group lighting, climate, media and other functions coherently. Preserve the user's device names. Show relevant room context, such as a temperature with its unit and current state.

Use the same device-control pattern in every room. A lamp should not require a tap in one room, a hidden long press in another and a different custom action elsewhere without a real reason.

## Images and icons

Room images can help recognition. Device icons support quick scanning. Both supplement the room and device names. Keep sensor values and controls outside busy imagery.

## Responsive and shared use

Allow scrolling and a single-column layout. A wall-mounted display and a phone may need different card arrangements, but they should use the same labels, states and control meanings.

ONS [dashboard guidance](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/guidance/dashboards) supports prominent important insights, written context and scrolling rather than forcing all information into one screen. NHS [cards](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/design-system/components/card) support related content groups.
